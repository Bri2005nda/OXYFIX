Abstract—A recent model built on composition-based de-
scriptors (CKSAAP) of nitrogenase proteins (NifH, NifD, NifK)
reported an accuracy of 0.92 and an AUC of 0.98 for classifying
diazotrophs as aerobic or anaerobic, and interpreted its features
as determinants of oxygen tolerance. Because oxygen niche is
strongly conserved within bacterial clades, such scores may reflect
lineage recognition rather than any oxygen-related property
of nitrogenase. We re-evaluated this task on 787 diazotrophic
strains (356 aerobic, 431 anaerobic) under a protocol designed
to make that question decidable: a label-provenance audit against
BacDive; matched negative-control proteins with no expected link
to oxygen tolerance (RecA, GyrB, RpoB) through the identical
pipeline; splits of increasing strictness (random, genus-, family-
and order-disjoint, leave-one-genus-out) repeated 20 times with
bootstrap intervals and a permutation test; and two reference
baselines, a phylogenetic nearest neighbour on GTDB distances
and 22 trivial covariates. Only 489 of 787 labels (62%) were
supported by a matching BacDive record. Under a random
split, nitrogenase (AUC 0.974 ± 0.007) was indistinguishable
from housekeeping controls ( 0.973–0.982); under genus-disjoint
splitting a small advantage appeared ( 0.945 ± 0.030 vs. 0.911–
0.916); under leave-one-genus-out both target and control re-
turned to chance (0.549 [0.367, 0.753] and 0.560), on the only
8 of 328 genera that contain both classes. The phylogenetic
nearest neighbour alone reached 0.972, and 22 trivial covariates
reached 0.96, leaving about one AUC point for the 1 600 CKSAAP
dimensions; frozen ESM-2 650M embeddings did not reverse
the comparison, the RecA control scoring above the target. In
this benchmark, the reported accuracy is reproducible but is
explained by phylogenetic structure rather than by an oxygen-
specific signal in nitrogenase; a weak target-specific component
of 0.03–0.06 AUC remains neither confirmed nor excluded. We
propose a reusable five-item minimum evaluation standard for

I. I NTRODUCTION
A. Sequence-based phenotype prediction has outrun its own
evaluation standards
Predicting microbial phenotypes directly from sequence is
now routine, with reported accuracies above 0.90 for oxygen
requirement, temperature and pH optima, carbon-source use
and many other traits. Oxygen requirement is among the most
frequently modelled because it is biologically fundamental and
because BacDive provides curated annotations at scale [1].
The difficulty is that almost every microbial trait of eco-
logical interest is phylogenetically structured. Martiny et
al. showed with the consenTRAIT metric that 93% of 89
functional traits examined were non-randomly distributed
across the bacterial and archaeal tree [2]. Any sequence-
derived feature — amino-acid composition, k-mers, gene
presence/absence, language-model embeddings — also carries
phylogenetic signal, because sequences are inherited verti-
cally. When a conserved trait is predicted from a phylogeny-
correlated feature set under a random train/test split, the model
can score very well simply by recognising which clade a
sequence belongs to.
The better papers in this area already guard against
this: Flamholz et al. withhold whole taxonomic groups [3],
GenomeSPOT applies phylogenetic balancing and family-level

===== PAGE 2 =====
partitioning [4], and Liao et al. compare curated oxygen-
related gene families against phylogenetically selected ones
[5] — a comparison between gene sets, not between a target
protein and a matched housekeeping protein from the same
strain. These safeguards are applied unevenly: a substantial
body of applied work, particularly work that targets a specific
protein family and interprets feature importances mechanisti-
cally, still reports random-split performance as if it measured
a biological signal.
B. The specific case: oxygen niche predicted from nitrogenase
sequences
Nitrogenase is irreversibly inactivated by oxygen within
minutes, yet diazotrophs occupy the full range of oxygen
niches, from obligate anaerobes to the obligate aerobe Azoto-
bacter vinelandii. Aerobic diazotrophs solve the conflict with
protection strategies that are largely not encoded in the nitroge-
nase structural subunits: respiratory protection, conformational
protection by the Shethna FeSII protein, heterocyst differ-
entiation, temporal separation, and leghaemoglobin-buffered
microaerobiosis in legume nodules. Comparative genomics
places the signal in the accessory repertoire: NifW, NifX/NafY
and NifZ are essential for diazotrophic growth in Leptolyngbya
boryana whereas HesA and HesB are not [6], and machine-
learning work on Anabaena concentrates FOX (fixation in the
presence of oxygen) gene candidates in envelope, redox and
metallocluster-maturation functions rather than in NifHDK [7].
There is nevertheless a real, documented structural correlate
inside nitrogenase itself: Cuevas-Zuvir ´ıa et al. report that the
NifK N-terminal extension is predominantly found in oxygen-
tolerant aerobic and facultative diazotrophs, while a NifD
insertion characteristic of Nif-II is mostly found in anaerobes
[8]. That observation is itself clade-structured, which sharpens
the identifiability question rather than settling it.
Against this background, a prior Master’s thesis from our
laboratory [9] trained composition-based descriptors (CK-
SAAP [10], computed with iFeatureOmega [11]) of NifH,
NifD and NifK on 787 diazotrophic strains and reported
an accuracy of 0.9225 and an AUC of 0.9772 for aerobic-
versus-anaerobic classification under a random split, with
SHAP analysis [12] interpreted as pointing to physicochemical
determinants of oxygen tolerance. Related recent models —
Carmna [13] and NFEmbed [14] for nitrogenase activity —
follow the same template: protein features of a nif -related
target, a classical or embedding-based classifier, SHAP inter-
pretation, random splits.
C. The gap
None of this applied work answers the question that de-
cides what such a model means: does the sequence of the
nitrogenase structural subunits carry information about
oxygen niche beyond what any conserved protein from the
same organism carries? The question is cheaply answerable
and has not been asked for this trait. Four components are
missing. Matched protein negative controls : the right control
is not a shuffled label but the same pipeline applied to an
unrelated protein from the same strain, and if a housekeeping
protein predicts oxygen niche as well as nitrogenase does,
the model is reading the organism rather than the enzyme.
A taxonomic decay curve rather than a single split , which
turns the confound into a measurable quantity. Reference
baselines that cost nothing — a nearest-neighbour classifier
on a published phylogeny, and a handful of trivial covariates
— which bound what a sequence descriptor may claim. Label
provenance as a reported quantity : BacDive lists more than
one oxygen state for 7.1% of strains [15], and the usual
diazotroph label conflates the organism’s oxygen relationship
with the conditions under which it fixes nitrogen — rhizobia
are aerobes that fix in microaerobic nodules, Azospirillum fixes
under reduced O 2, heterocystous cyanobacteria are aerobes
whose fixing cell is anoxic. Papers in this area report a strain
count, not an evidence level.
D. Contributions
This paper is a controlled re-evaluation, not a performance
paper. We make five contributions.
(i) A label-provenance audit of a published diazotroph
dataset, traced strain by strain against BacDive and released
as a provenance table, filtering rather than rewriting to produce
a 489-strain documented subset (Section IV-A). (ii) The first
matched-housekeeping-protein control for a nitrogenase-
based trait model , repeated over 20 splits with bootstrap in-
tervals and a two-null permutation test (Sections IV-B, IV-C).
(iii) A taxonomic decay curve over random, genus-, family-
and order-disjoint and leave-one-genus-out protocols, which
locates where the reported performance lives (Section IV-D).
(iv) Reference baselines and a richer representation : a
GTDB nearest-neighbour classifier, 22 trivial covariates and
frozen ESM-2 650M embeddings, all under the identical
protocol, which jointly bound the contribution of the sequence
descriptor and rule out the objection that the diagnosis is an
artefact of a weak feature set (Sections IV-E, IV-F). (v) A
transferable minimum evaluation standard for phylogenet-
ically conserved microbial traits, stated as a five-item protocol
(Section V-D).
We also report an instructive negative lesson about the
protocol itself: the strength of a taxonomic or sequence-
identity split is a property of the corpus, not only of the
splitting rule. In this corpus 98% of genera, 89% of families
and 95% of orders are single-class, and MMseqs2 clustering at
30% identity leaves a single giant component, so withholding
a group removes almost no decision boundary while scores
stay high.

III. M ATERIALS AND METHODS
Fig. 1 summarises the design.
A. Strain set and phenotype labels
We reuse the strain collection of the prior study [9]: 787
diazotrophic bacterial strains with binary oxygen-relationship
labels (356 aerobic, 431 anaerobic). Reusing the exact collec-
tion is deliberate — the question is what that dataset supports,
so changing it would make the comparison uninterpretable.
A direct recount of the source FASTA files gives 6,710
sequences (3,426 aerobic, 3,284 anaerobic), matching one of
the two inherited counts exactly; the second inherited count
(6,335) differs by 375 sequences with no recorded filter, and
is reported as unresolved rather than reconciled by conjecture.
The label is the organism’s oxygen relationship as recorded
in the source study, not an assay of nitrogen fixation under
defined pO 2. This conflation is a known weakness of the trait
definition and we return to it in Section V-C.
B. Label provenance audit
Each strain was queried against BacDive [1] by strain
name; a BacDive identifier was obtained for 711 strains and
an oxygen-tolerance string recovered verbatim for 678. The
retrieved field was compared with the inherited label, giving
three classes. Documented — a record exists and its oxygen
state agrees with the inherited label; retained in the Strict
corpus. To verify — a record exists but states a different or
intermediate category (facultative anaerobe, microaerophile),
or multiple conflicting states; excluded. Missing — no record
retrieved; excluded.
We filter; we never rewrite a label. A strain whose Bac-
Dive entry reads “facultative anaerobe” against an inherited
“aerobic” is removed rather than relabelled, because its true
state under nitrogen-fixing conditions is unresolved by the
evidence at hand. From the same strings we also derived
a four-level oxygen-relationship label (aerobic, facultative,
microaerophilic, anaerobic), used here descriptively.
C. Target and control proteins
Target. NifH, NifD and NifK protein sequences per strain,
as in the source study, evaluated jointly and, for the ablation
of Section IV-E, one subunit at a time.
Controls. RecA, GyrB and RpoB retrieved from UniProt
[16] for the same strains. They are present in essentially
all bacteria, widely used as phylogenetic marker genes, and
without established mechanistic link to oxygen tolerance. The
second property is the point: they carry taxonomic signal
and nothing else relevant to the trait, so they measure what
a phylogeny-driven classifier can achieve here. A control
that matches the target is not a failed control — it is the
measurement.
D. Representations and baselines
CKSAAP [10], computed with iFeatureOmegaCLI [11] un-
der the settings of the source study, yielding 1,600 dimensions
per protein; the target/control comparison therefore differs in
the protein and in nothing else. ESM-2 650M [20], frozen,
residue vectors mean-pooled per sequence and then per strain,
under the strictly identical downstream protocol. GTDB near-
est neighbour: a k-NN classifier ( k = 5) on patristic distances
from the GTDB reference tree [22], 746 of 787 strains matched
— a model that sees relatedness and nothing else. Trivial

E. Split protocols
Random: stratified 80/20, a genus may appear on both
sides — the protocol of the source study and most
comparable papers. Genus-, family- and order-disjoint :
GroupShuffleSplit grouped on the GTDB rank. Leave-
one-genus-out (LOGO): one entire genus forms the test set;
because AUC is undefined on a single-class test set, LOGO
is evaluated only on genera containing both classes, and per-
genus AUCs are reported rather than pooled, pooling being
exactly the between-genus comparison the protocol exists to
block. Sequence-identity clustering with MMseqs2 [21] at 30%
and 50%, corpus-wide and protein by protein.
F . Models, imbalance and statistics
Five classifier families were compared — histogram-based
gradient boosting (HGB), XGBoost [17], ExtraTrees, Random
Forest and a multilayer perceptron — with scikit-learn [18].
This is not a search for the best model: it tests whether the
diagnosis depends on the learner. HGB is used throughout
unless stated. Four treatments of class imbalance were com-
pared: SMOTE [19] on training folds only, no resampling,
class weighting, and scale_pos_weight.
Every configuration was repeated over 20 splits (seeds 42–
61) and is reported as mean ± SD, with MCC and AUPRC
alongside AUC and accuracy, and bootstrap 95% confidence
intervals (10,000 resamples). A permutation test (1,000 permu-
tations) was run under two nulls: a global label shuffle, and a
shuffle that permutes labels between genus blocks, preserving
the taxonomic structure of the labels.
G. Availability
Code, the provenance table and all result tables are de-
posited on Zenodo with a DOI and mirrored on GitHub, with
a frozen environment specification.

IV. R ESULTS
A. Only 62% of labels are supported by an independent record
Of 787 strains, 489 (62.1%) had a BacDive record agreeing
with the inherited label; 221 (28.1%) had a record that
disagreed or reported an intermediate state; 77 (9.8%) had
no retrievable record (Table II).
The attrition is strongly class-asymmetric: 58% of aero-
bic strains (206 of 356) fail documentation, against 21%
of anaerobic strains (92 of 431). This is not random noise
but the biology of the trait: the ambiguous categories —
facultative anaerobe, microaerophile — sit between “aerobic”
and “anaerobic”, and the organisms that occupy them are
disproportionately those labelled aerobic in a binary scheme.
Acidithiobacillus ferrianus (BacDive 160797), inherited as
aerobic and recorded as a facultative anaerobe, is a typical
exclusion. The four-level label makes the cost explicit: on the
documented subset the intermediate levels are empty by con-
struction, so curation removes precisely the physiologies most
informative about nitrogenase protection. The excluded strains
are not biologically defective but evidentially unresolved under
a binary label.
B. Housekeeping proteins predict oxygen niche as well as
nitrogenase
Under a random split repeated 20 times, all four proteins lie
within 0.009 AUC of each other on the Full corpus and within
0.004 on the Strict corpus, and in both cases the best score
belongs to a control (Table III, Fig. 2a,b). The target–control
gap is smaller than one standard deviation across splits.
The pipeline reproduces the published performance level,
and reproduces it just as well on proteins with no mechanistic

D. The taxonomic decay curve, and where it stops being
informative
Under splits of increasing taxonomic rank on the Full
corpus, nitrogenase AUC falls from 0.974 ± 0.007 (random)
to 0.945 ± 0.030 (genus), 0.946 (family) and 0.910 (order),
with RecA tracking below it throughout (Fig. 4a). The decline
is real but shallow, and the shallowness is itself the finding:
in this corpus 89% of families and 95% of orders contain a
single class, so removing an entire group removes almost no
decision boundary. A split that looks strict on paper can be
nearly free in practice.

both classes and are evaluable; on the Strict corpus none do, so

[0.367, 0.753], and 0.560 for RecA (Fig. 4b). The reading is

support a conclusion in either direction.
E. Phylogeny alone, and 22 numbers, reproduce most of the
score
A k-nearest-neighbour classifier on GTDB patristic dis-
tances — a model with no access to any sequence feature
— reaches AUC 0.972 under a random split on the Full
corpus, against 0.974 for the full CKSAAP model (Fig. 5a).
Relatedness alone reproduces essentially the whole of the
reported performance.
The second baseline bounds the descriptor from the other
side: 22 trivial covariates reach 0.957, so the 1,600 CKSAAP
dimensions buy about one point of AUC (Fig. 5b). Finally,
NifH, NifD and NifK taken separately perform comparably to
their combination, with no subunit standing out — difficult
to reconcile with a mechanistic reading in which a specific
structural element carries the signal [8].
F . A protein language model reads the organism better, not
the trait
The obvious objection to everything above is that CKSAAP
is a crude descriptor and that a modern representation would
separate trait from lineage. It does not. Frozen ESM-2 650M
embeddings [20] under the identical protocol give, for the
target on the Full corpus, AUC 0.946 (random) and 0.915
(genus-disjoint), against 0.974 and 0.945 for CKSAAP —
lower on both. More tellingly, under a random split the
RecA control embedded by ESM-2 reaches 0.965, above the
nitrogenase target at 0.946 (Fig. 5c). A richer representation
reads organism identity better still, without separating trait
signal from phylogenetic signal. The diagnosis is a property
of the task and the corpus, not of the descriptor.
G. The hardness of a split is a property of the corpus
Three independent observations converge on the same point
(Fig. 4c). First, 98% of genera in the Full corpus and all genera
in the Strict corpus are single-class, so group-disjoint splitting
at genus rank withholds mostly homogeneous blocks; 89% of
families and 95% of orders behave likewise. Second, only 8
genera support a LOGO evaluation at all. Third, sequence-
identity clustering fails for a structural reason rather than a
technical one: MMseqs2 run over all 6,710 sequences at 30%
and 50% identity yields a giant component containing 99.9%
of strains, because the conserved domains shared by these
proteins link nearly every strain to every other. Rebuilding
the clustering protein by protein (NifH, NifD, NifK, RecA
separately) at 30% identity still leaves only one to three
inseparable components; the 50% evaluation is in progress.
We therefore report no performance figures from the clustered
protocol.
This is also what resolves an artefact we reported from a
single split in an earlier version of this analysis, where genus-
disjoint AUC on the Strict corpus exceeded random-split AUC.
Under 20 repetitions the inversion disappears ( 0.970 ± 0.025
against 0.982 ± 0.015), but the underlying cause it pointed to
is confirmed by the group-composition statistics: on a curated
subset in which every genus is single-class, genus-disjoint
splitting does not block the confound at all.
H. Nothing depends on the learner or on the imbalance
treatment
On the Full corpus under a random split, accuracy is 0.886
(ExtraTrees), 0.873 (Random Forest), 0.899 (XGBoost) and
0.911 (HGB), all near the published 0.9225 reference, and
the spread between best and worst learner is smaller than the
drop any single learner suffers from hardening the split. The
four imbalance treatments — SMOTE, no resampling, class
weighting and scale_pos_weight — give AUCs identical
to the third decimal, so SMOTE was dropped on this near-
balanced corpus. The limiting factor is the protocol, not the
learner or the sampler.
V. D ISCUSSION
A. What the evidence supports, and what it does not
Four claims are supported. The published performance
level is reproducible and is not specific to nitrogenase: three
proteins with no mechanistic connection to oxygen tolerance
match or exceed the target under random splitting, on both
corpora and under two representations. The performance is
contingent on the evaluation protocol : it declines monoton-
ically with the strictness of the split, and generalisation to an
unseen mixed genus is not demonstrated. Trivial baselines
bound what the descriptor may claim : relatedness alone
reaches 0.972 and 22 covariates reach 0.957. The hardness

Two claims are explicitly not supported in either direction.
We have not shown that nitrogenase sequence carries no
oxygen-related information: the target leads the controls by
0.03–0.06 AUC under repeated genus-disjoint splitting and
under nested cross-validation, which is compatible with a weak
specific component, and the association of the NifK N-terminal
extension with oxygen-tolerant diazotrophs [8] supplies a
mechanism by which one could exist. Nor have we shown
that such a model fails on an unseen genus: we have shown
that this corpus cannot answer the question, eight evaluable
genera being too few.
B. Relation to prior work
Our finding is no surprise to the part of the field that
already controls for phylogeny [3], [4], [5]. What is new
is threefold. (i) The matched-housekeeping-protein control
has, to our knowledge, not been applied to a nitrogenase-
based or any protein-family-targeted trait model, and it is
a sharper instrument than a phylogenetic split, because it
does not require the split to work. (ii) The decay curve with
repetitions and a two-null permutation test turns “there is some
confounding” into a number on the same model and corpus,
and separates significance from specificity. (iii) The corpus-
structure analysis identifies a practical trap we have not seen
reported: a group-aware split that silently stops working once
the groups are mostly single-class, which will recur wherever
a conserved trait is curated down to a cleaner subset.
The contrast with Flamholz et al. remains instructive:
their annotation-free classifiers retained better-than-random
accuracy on withheld groups, whereas ours return to chance
at LOGO. Whole-proteome composition integrates genuinely
physiological signal [4], whereas three structural subunits do
not. The trait signal recoverable from sequence depends on
how much of the organism you look at , and narrowing to
the mechanistically “obvious” protein can remove the signal
rather than concentrate it.
C. Limitations
The label conflates two things : the trait is the organism’s
oxygen relationship, not the conditions under which it fixes
nitrogen, and for rhizobia, Azospirillum and heterocystous
cyanobacteria these differ. The four-level label derived here
describes the organism, not the fixation condition; a fixation-
condition label with recorded evidence type, strain by strain,
is the correct target and is in preparation. The corpus bounds
the strictest protocol : with 8 evaluable genera, LOGO yields
an interval too wide to rank anything, and sequence-identity
clustering yields no usable partition at all. No external vali-
dation: everything is internal to one strain collection, so the
conclusions are about what this widely reused dataset supports.
Class imbalance introduced by curation : the filter removed
aerobes preferentially, making the dataset cleaner and less
balanced at once, and it removed the intermediate physiologies
entirely. One sequence-count discrepancy remains open (6,710
against an inherited 6,335). Finally, the negative controls
are not perfect negatives : amino-acid usage in housekeeping
proteins responds weakly to aerobic metabolism, so the con-
trol bounds the phylogenetic contribution slightly generously.
This works against us — the true phylogeny-only baseline
may be marginally lower, leaving slightly more room for a
nitrogenase-specific signal than we report.
D. A minimum evaluation standard
The protocol used here is cheap and generalises to any
phylogenetically conserved microbial trait. We state it as five
requirements.
(1) Report label provenance, not just sample size: how
many labels are supported by an independent record, how
many conflict, how many are absent, and whether attrition
is class-dependent. (2) Run a matched control protein:
a conserved protein with no mechanistic link to the trait,

===== PAGE 8 =====
from the same organisms, through the identical pipeline; if
it matches the target, the target is not the source of the
signal. This is a few hours of work and it is diagnostic. (3)
Report a decay curve with repetitions, not a single split:
random, group-disjoint and leave-one-group-out at minimum,
with means, dispersions and intervals — the shape of the
curve, not any single point, is the result, and in our case
a single draw overstated the target–control gap by a factor
of four. (4) Verify that the hard split is actually hard:
report the class composition of the held-out groups, because
if most are single-class, group-disjoint splitting does not block
the confound and must not be reported as though it did. (5)
Report the two free baselines: a nearest-neighbour classifier
on a published phylogeny, and a handful of trivial covariates.
If either matches the proposed model, the proposed model has
not been shown to add anything.
The cost in our case was a few weeks of compute and
curation; the benefit was learning that the headline number
meant something different from what it appeared to mean,
which unchecked would have propagated into an interpretation
of SHAP values as oxygen-protection determinants. Two im-
plications follow. For diazotroph biology, if oxygen tolerance
is to be predicted from sequence, the accessory repertoire
— fesII, cydAB, cox, fdxH, rnf, vnf, nifW, hetR/patS — is
the place to look, not the structural subunits [6], [7]. For
the machine-learning side, a frozen protein language model
reading lineage better than a composition descriptor is a
caution worth generalising: representation quality and trait
specificity are different axes, and improving the former can
worsen the identifiability problem rather than solve it.
VI. C ONCLUSION
A sequence-composition model that classifies diazotroph
oxygen niche from nitrogenase with AUC 0.974 under a
random split is matched by housekeeping proteins run through
the identical pipeline, is nearly equalled by a classifier that
sees only phylogenetic distance, and declines steadily as splits
harden until, on the only eight genera that permit the test, it is
indistinguishable from chance. Replacing the descriptor with a
protein language model makes the problem worse, not better.
The performance is real; its interpretation as a mechanistic
signal in nitrogenase is not supported, and a weak specific
component of a few AUC points can be neither confirmed
nor excluded by this corpus. We do not claim nitrogenase
sequence is uninformative about oxygen niche — we claim
the published experiments cannot tell, and we give a cheap
five-item protocol that can.
